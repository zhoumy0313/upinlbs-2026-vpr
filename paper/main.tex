\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Development draft; the final abstract will follow held-out evaluation.}
A second visual encoder can correct retrieval errors but can also damage
correct predictions. We examine whether estimating correction minus damage
is more effective than predicting the first encoder's error under an
invocation budget. Frozen CosPlace descriptors supply five similarity
statistics to small supervised logistic heads. A selected second encoder
performs independent global retrieval only after the gate decision.
On 300 development queries from Nordland-Part-2020, an initial DINOv2 CLS
model is weaker than CosPlace, and the benefit policy makes no second-stage
calls. With the sole predeclared EigenPlaces replacement, 25\% invocation
increases Recall@1 from 71.33\% to 75.67\%, below the same-input error
predictor's 77.33\%. These findings motivate a prospective comparison of
routing targets and failure modes rather than a claim that benefit
prediction is already superior.

\section{Introduction}
Visual place recognition (VPR) retrieves previously observed places from
an image database. Its candidates can support relocalization or loop
closure, but retrieval alone does not establish a geometrically verified
pose~\cite{tutorial}. Appearance change motivates using a second model
when a fast descriptor struggles. The benefit depends on how the models'
errors overlap: a difficult query may defeat both models, and replacing a
correct fast prediction can introduce an error.

We ask a restricted operational question: before computing a second-model
query descriptor, can five statistics from the first model's scores guide
useful additional calls? We compare score-gap invocation, random selection,
supervised error prediction, and supervised correction minus damage.
The encoders remain frozen, but the selector is trained. A triggered query
receives the second model's whole global ranking, without score fusion,
temporal matching, local reranking or geometric verification.

Uncertainty estimation and predicting postprocessing effects have prior
work. Our intended contribution is an auditable empirical comparison
under the same available information, including a weaker second model,
a stronger specialist, and actual conditional execution. Development
results do not establish superiority of the benefit target. Prospective
evaluation will examine a held-out route block and a different day--night
collection without selecting models or thresholds from their results.

\section{Related Work}
CosPlace~\cite{cosplace} and EigenPlaces~\cite{eigenplaces} provide
specialist global descriptors. We also examine DINOv2's~\cite{dinov2}
normalized global CLS output, a general visual representation; this
adapter is not AnyLoc or SALAD. Our fixed low-resolution condition is
not a reproduction of the original papers' benchmark numbers.

Score-based uncertainty, including Statistical Uncertainty
(SU)~\cite{doubt}, estimates match reliability. Selective image
matching~\cite{match} uses matching evidence to study when postprocessing
helps. Sequence-matching receptiveness prediction~\cite{smr} already
distinguishes beneficial and harmful sequence-processing effects. We do
not introduce correction/damage labels. Our gate observes a single
query's first-encoder score statistics and selects a second global
encoder before computing its query output. We inspected the available
selective-matching and SMR code. A numerical SU comparator is omitted
because its official implementation and complete numerical conventions
have not been verified; this limits the comparison.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Retrieval and Available Information}
Let $D=\{d_j\}_{j=1}^{N}$ be an ordered gallery. Frozen encoder $m$ produces
$z_m(I)=f_m(I)/\|f_m(I)\|_2$, and exact cosine similarity is
\begin{equation}
s_m(q_i,d_j)=z_m(q_i)^\top z_m(d_j).
\end{equation}
Candidates are ordered by decreasing score; ties retain manifest order.
Ground-truth aligned frames are used only for training labels and
evaluation. Frame indices never enter the online gate and are not treated
as geographic coordinates.

For sorted A scores $s_{(1)}\geq\cdots\geq s_{(N)}$, the input is
\begin{equation}
u=\big[s_{(1)},\ s_{(1)}-s_{(2)},\ s_{(1)}-s_{(5)},\
\mu_{10},\ \sigma_{10}\big]^\top,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{10}$ and $\sigma_{10}$ are the top-ten mean and population
standard deviation. Retained features are standardized using training
queries only. B outputs are unavailable at the decision point.

\subsection{Supervised Correction and Damage Targets}
Let $a_i,b_i\in\{0,1\}$ denote top-one correctness on training queries.
Construct
\begin{equation}
y_i^+=\ind[a_i=0,b_i=1],\qquad y_i^-=\ind[a_i=1,b_i=0].
\end{equation}
Two logistic heads give estimates $p_+(u),p_-(u)$ and
\begin{equation}
g(u)=p_+(u)-p_-(u).
\end{equation}
For exact conditional probabilities, the difference equals
$\mathbb{E}[b-a\mid u]$. Our regularized estimates are not certified
probabilities or a guarantee of benefit. Both heads use $C=1$, LBFGS,
no class weights, seed~0 and at most 2000 iterations; convergence is
checked. The primary error control has error/correct heads with the same
features and regularization. Complementary targets yield the same ordering
as a single error head, also reported. Equal head counts do not create
independent information.

\subsection{Budget Thresholds and Ranking Selection}
For validation budgets $\beta\in\{0.10,0.25,0.50\}$, a threshold allows at
most $\lfloor\beta Q_{\rm val}\rfloor$ calls. Under strict $g>t$, we use
an adjacent selected/unselected score midpoint, with conservative ties.
The benefit threshold is clipped to at least zero, so a budget need not
be filled when estimated net gain is negative. This numerical boundary
handling was corrected during development after detecting batch-versus-
single-query roundoff effects; original runs remain available.

If triggered, B retrieves over the full gallery and replaces A's entire
ranking; otherwise A is returned (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Thresholds are
fixed on final queries; the final invocation fraction may differ from the
validation budget. For $Q$ evaluated queries,
\begin{equation}
R@1_{\rm route}-R@1_A=(N_{\rm rescue}-N_{\rm harm})/Q.
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data and Leakage Control}
Nordland-Part-2020~\cite{nordland} contains 1750 aligned summer and winter
frames. All summer frames form the gallery. One-based winter ordinals
1--600 train the gate, 701--1000 select validation thresholds, and
1101--1750 are the planned 650-query holdout. Blocks 601--700 and
1001--1100 are unused buffers. Numeric filenames are sorted before
assigning ordinals; they start at 251, so ordinals differ from raw filename
numbers. Primary positives are references within five aligned frames;
radii one and ten are planned sensitivity analyses using unchanged
rankings and decisions. This is a fixed-map route-block holdout, not an
independent unseen route.

The planned external test uses Gardens Point's 200 day-right references
and 200 night-right queries. Exactly aligned frames are the primary
positives. The tutorial's soft ground truth is an ambiguity/ignore mask;
our radius-eight positives are explicitly a custom tolerance analysis.
Full decoding, ID order, alignment and duplicate hashes were checked.
Repeated images remain in their original splits; none are removed to
improve results.

\subsection{Models and Controls}
A is CosPlace ResNet18/512. Initial B is DINOv2 ViT-S/14 with a normalized
384-dimensional final CLS output and no register tokens. After retaining
its development failure, the single predeclared replacement is
EigenPlaces ResNet50/512. All models use RGB, ImageNet normalization,
and antialiased bilinear tensor resizing to $224\times224$. The full-image
warp is a declared adaptation, not DINOv2's original classification crop.
EigenPlaces retains official modules and pooling; a redundant constructor
initializer download is bypassed before strict loading of the full
checkpoint. Loaded tensors, source commits, hashes and row IDs are
recorded. TF32 and cuDNN benchmarking are disabled.

Controls comprise A-only, B-only, random invocation, top1--top2 gap,
the same-input A-error predictor and benefit prediction. Ablations remove
the harm head or the top-ten standard deviation. Random seeds 0--4 are
all reported as means and population standard deviations. Random costs
are validation-matched only; final actual fractions may differ.
A ground-truth oracle is diagnostic and never deployed. Recall@$K$,
$K=1,5,10$, counts queries with a positive in their first $K$ candidates.
We also report invocation, rescues, harms and signed Recall gain.

\subsection{Actual Runtime}
The development runtime uses an RTX 3060 Laptop GPU, PyTorch 2.6.0+cu124,
batch one, twenty warmup queries and three complete validation passes.
CUDA is synchronized around each query. Times include warm-file decode,
RGB, preprocessing, selected encoders, gate and exact retrieval. Model
loading, gallery building and reference checks are excluded and accounted
for separately. Cached accuracy simulation is not model latency.
The unlocked GPU clock limits fine timing comparisons.

\section{Development Findings}
\subsection{Complementarity}
Validation A reaches 71.33\% R@1 and initial DINOv2 51.67\%. B can rescue
29 errors and damage 88 successes among 300 queries; the diagnostic oracle
is 81.00\%. The benefit policy calls B zero times at all budgets, retaining
A accuracy. This is not an accuracy improvement. At 10\%, error prediction
rescues eight and damages four, reaching 72.67\%. The original model pair
is retained after examining the alternative.

EigenPlaces reaches 83.33\% R@1, with 51 rescue and 15 harm opportunities;
its oracle is 88.33\%. Training A/B R@1 are 82.50\%/86.67\%, with 59 rescue
and 34 harm opportunities among 600 queries. The different balance across
blocks cautions against inferring transfer from the fitted estimates.

\subsection{Correction and Real Execution}
At 25\%, benefit prediction rescues 18 queries and damages five, improving
A by 4.33 points. The error predictor rescues 22 and damages four, improving
by 6.00 points. Validation-matched random selection averages 73.87\% R@1
with a 1.34-point population standard deviation. At 50\%, benefit prediction
avoids three harms relative to error prediction but loses three rescues;
both reach 82.00\%. Less damage alone does not demonstrate higher net
accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:development}).

Measured validation mean times for the EigenPlaces pair are 8.92\,ms
(A-only), 14.44\,ms (B-only), 13.02\,ms (25\% benefit) and 13.38\,ms
(25\% error). Each conditional policy performs 900 A forwards and 225 B
forwards over three 300-query passes. Every decision and ranking matches
the frozen reference, checked outside the timer. For the initial pair,
zero-call benefit routing takes 10.19\,ms versus A-only's 7.78\,ms: fewer
second-model calls do not eliminate selector cost. These sequential laptop
measurements are not confidence intervals for small timing differences.

\section{Discussion and Next Evaluation}
Current evidence supports neither universal second-encoder benefit nor
superiority of the benefit target. The stronger specialist creates more
rescue opportunities, while error prediction remains competitive. Final
evaluation must establish transfer and compare actual cost with simply
using that specialist. Negative transfer will remain part of the study,
not trigger another model search.

Limitations include small aligned collections, fixed-route dependence,
a five-feature linear selector, domain shift, unlocked laptop clocks and
the missing verified SU comparator. This work does not demonstrate open-set
rejection, metric pose accuracy, SLAM improvement or robot deployment.
