\begin{abstract}
A second visual encoder can correct retrieval errors, damage correct
matches, or spend computation on queries both models miss. We compare
predicting correction minus damage with predicting fast-model error,
using five first-encoder similarity statistics and frozen global
descriptors. Models and validation thresholds are fixed before evaluating
650 Nordland holdout queries and 200 Gardens Point night queries.
With EigenPlaces as the second encoder, a nominal 25\% validation budget
transfers to 78.92\% invocation on the holdout and 15.50\% externally.
Holdout Recall@1 falls from CosPlace's 14.15\% to 10.31\%; external
Recall@1 stays at 34.50\%. With the original, weaker DINOv2 CLS model,
benefit routing largely avoids harmful holdout calls, whereas error
prediction damages accuracy. Across 107100 real online query records,
invocation alone does not determine total latency. These results support
a limited empirical conclusion: routing targets must account for model
complementarity, but a small benefit predictor does not guarantee accuracy
gain or a transferable budget. We report failures, frame-tolerance
sensitivity and data ambiguity rather than universal superiority.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
visual place recognition, conditional computation, retrieval, evaluation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Visual place recognition (VPR) retrieves previously observed places from
an image database. Its candidates can support relocalization or loop
closure, but retrieval alone does not establish a geometrically verified
pose~\cite{tutorial}. Appearance change motivates using a second model
when a fast descriptor struggles. The benefit depends on how the models'
errors overlap: a difficult query may defeat both models, and replacing a
correct fast prediction can introduce an error.

We ask a restricted operational question: before computing a second-model
query descriptor, can five statistics from the first model's scores guide
useful additional calls? We compare score-gap invocation, random selection,
supervised error prediction, and supervised correction minus damage.
The encoders remain frozen, but the selector is trained. A triggered query
receives the second model's whole global ranking, without score fusion,
temporal matching, local reranking or geometric verification.

Uncertainty estimation and predicting postprocessing effects have prior
work. Our contribution is a constrained empirical comparison under the
same available information, preserving a weaker second model and the
single development-selected specialist replacement. We examine three
failure modes: changed correction/damage opportunities, transfer of
validation budgets, and discrepancy between invocation and real execution
cost. Both final datasets use frozen models and thresholds; their results
do not select another encoder or a revised gate.

\section{Related Work}
CosPlace~\cite{cosplace} and EigenPlaces~\cite{eigenplaces} provide
specialist global descriptors. We also examine DINOv2's~\cite{dinov2}
normalized global CLS output, a general visual representation. Our fixed low-resolution condition is
not a reproduction of the original papers' benchmark numbers.

Score-based uncertainty, including Statistical Uncertainty
(SU)~\cite{doubt}, estimates match reliability. Selective image
matching~\cite{match} uses matching evidence to study when postprocessing
helps. Sequence-matching receptiveness prediction~\cite{smr} already
distinguishes beneficial and harmful sequence-processing effects. We do
not introduce correction/damage labels. Our gate observes a single
query's first-encoder score statistics and selects a second global
encoder before computing its query output. We inspected the available
selective-matching and SMR code. A numerical SU comparator is omitted
because its official implementation and complete numerical conventions
have not been verified; this limits the comparison.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Retrieval and Available Information}
Let $D=\{d_j\}_{j=1}^{N}$ be an ordered gallery. Frozen encoder $m$ produces
$z_m(I)=f_m(I)/\|f_m(I)\|_2$, and exact cosine similarity is
\begin{equation}
s_m(q_i,d_j)=z_m(q_i)^\top z_m(d_j).
\end{equation}
Candidates are ordered by decreasing score; ties retain manifest order.
Ground-truth aligned frames are used only for training labels and
evaluation. Frame indices never enter the online gate and are not treated
as geographic coordinates.

For sorted A scores $s_{(1)}\geq\cdots\geq s_{(N)}$, the input is
\begin{equation}
u=\big[s_{(1)},\ s_{(1)}-s_{(2)},\ s_{(1)}-s_{(5)},\
\mu_{10},\ \sigma_{10}\big]^\top,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{10}$ and $\sigma_{10}$ are the top-ten mean and population
standard deviation. Retained features are standardized using training
queries only. B outputs are unavailable at the decision point.

\subsection{Supervised Correction and Damage Targets}
Let $a_i,b_i\in\{0,1\}$ denote top-one correctness on training queries.
Construct
\begin{equation}
y_i^+=\ind[a_i=0,b_i=1],\qquad y_i^-=\ind[a_i=1,b_i=0].
\end{equation}
Two logistic heads give estimates $p_+(u),p_-(u)$ and
\begin{equation}
g(u)=p_+(u)-p_-(u).
\end{equation}
For exact conditional probabilities, the difference equals
$\mathbb{E}[b-a\mid u]$. Our regularized estimates are not certified
probabilities or a guarantee of benefit. Both heads use $C=1$, LBFGS,
no class weights, seed~0 and at most 2000 iterations; convergence is
checked. The primary error control has error/correct heads with the same
features and regularization. Complementary targets yield the same ordering
as a single error head, also reported. Equal head counts do not create
independent information.

\subsection{Budget Thresholds and Ranking Selection}
For validation budgets $\beta\in\{0.10,0.25,0.50\}$, a threshold allows at
most $\lfloor\beta Q_{\rm val}\rfloor$ calls. Under strict $g>t$, we use
an adjacent selected/unselected score midpoint, with conservative ties.
The benefit threshold is clipped to at least zero, so a budget need not
be filled when estimated net gain is negative. This numerical boundary
handling was corrected during development after detecting batch-versus-
single-query roundoff effects; original runs remain available.

If triggered, B retrieves over the full gallery and replaces A's entire
ranking; otherwise A is returned (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Thresholds are
fixed on final queries; the final invocation fraction may differ from the
validation budget. For $Q$ evaluated queries,
\begin{equation}
R@1_{\rm route}-R@1_A=(N_{\rm rescue}-N_{\rm harm})/Q.
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Data and Leakage Control}
Nordland-Part-2020~\cite{nordland} provides 1750 summer and 1750 winter
frames with matching numeric IDs; we evaluate this supplied frame alignment. All summer frames form the gallery. One-based winter ordinals
1--600 train the gate, 701--1000 select validation thresholds, and
1101--1750 form the 650-query holdout. Blocks 601--700 and
1001--1100 are unused buffers. Numeric filenames are sorted before
assigning ordinals; they start at 251, so ordinals differ from raw filename
numbers. Primary positives are references within five aligned frames;
radii one and ten are sensitivity analyses using unchanged
rankings and decisions. This is a fixed-map route-block holdout, not an
independent unseen route.

The external test uses Gardens Point's 200 day-right references
and 200 night-right queries. Exactly aligned frames are the primary
positives. The tutorial's soft ground truth is an ambiguity/ignore mask;
our radius-eight positives are explicitly a custom tolerance analysis.
Full decoding, ID order, alignment and duplicate hashes were checked.
Repeated images remain in their original splits; none are removed to
improve results. In the holdout, 187 queries belong to 79 byte-identical
groups; 542 unique hashes remain among 650 records. Gardens Point has
one duplicated night-image pair. These dependencies limit interpretation
of fine frame-index errors and independent-sample claims.

\subsection{Models and Controls}
A is CosPlace ResNet18/512. Initial B is DINOv2 ViT-S/14 with a normalized
384-dimensional final CLS output and no register tokens. After retaining
its development failure, the single predeclared replacement is
EigenPlaces ResNet50/512. All models use RGB, ImageNet normalization,
and antialiased bilinear tensor resizing to $224\times224$. The full-image
warp is a declared adaptation, not DINOv2's original classification crop.
EigenPlaces retains official modules and pooling; a redundant constructor
initializer download is bypassed before strict loading of the full
checkpoint. Loaded tensors, source commits, hashes and row IDs are
recorded. TF32 and cuDNN benchmarking are disabled.

Controls comprise A-only, B-only, random invocation, top1--top2 gap,
the same-input A-error predictor and benefit prediction. Ablations remove
the harm head or the top-ten standard deviation. Random seeds 0--4 are
all reported as means and population standard deviations. Random costs
are validation-matched only; final actual fractions may differ.
A ground-truth oracle is diagnostic and never deployed. Recall@$K$,
$K=1,5,10$, counts queries with a positive in their first $K$ candidates.
We also report invocation, rescues, harms and signed Recall gain.

\subsection{Actual Runtime}
Measurements use an RTX 3060 Laptop GPU, PyTorch 2.6.0+cu124,
batch one, twenty warmup queries and three complete query passes.
CUDA is synchronized around each query. Times include warm-file decode,
RGB, preprocessing, selected encoders, gate and exact retrieval. Model
loading, gallery building and reference checks are excluded and accounted
for separately, using warm-file construction and index setup diagnostics.
Torch allocator peaks do not measure full-process memory; conditional
policies retain both encoders. Cached accuracy simulation is not model latency.
The unlocked GPU clock limits fine timing comparisons.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Complementarity}
Validation A reaches 71.33\% R@1 and initial DINOv2 51.67\%. B can rescue
29 errors and damage 88 successes among 300 queries; the diagnostic oracle
is 81.00\%. The benefit policy calls B zero times at all budgets, retaining
A accuracy. This is not an accuracy improvement. At 10\%, error prediction
rescues eight and damages four, reaching 72.67\%. The original model pair
is retained after examining the alternative.

EigenPlaces reaches 83.33\% R@1, with 51 rescue and 15 harm opportunities;
its oracle is 88.33\%. Training A/B R@1 are 82.50\%/86.67\%, with 59 rescue
and 34 harm opportunities among 600 queries. The different balance across
blocks cautions against inferring transfer from the fitted estimates.

\subsection{Correction and Real Execution}
At 25\%, benefit prediction rescues 18 queries and damages five, improving
A by 4.33 points. The error predictor rescues 22 and damages four, improving
by 6.00 points. Validation-matched random selection averages 73.87\% R@1
with a 1.34-point population standard deviation. At 50\%, benefit prediction
avoids three harms relative to error prediction but loses three rescues;
both reach 82.00\%. Less damage alone does not demonstrate higher net
accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:development}).

Measured validation mean times for the EigenPlaces pair are 8.92\,ms
(A-only), 14.44\,ms (B-only), 13.02\,ms (25\% benefit) and 13.38\,ms
(25\% error). Each conditional policy performs 900 A forwards and 225 B
forwards over three 300-query passes. Every decision and ranking matches
the frozen reference, checked outside the timer. For the initial pair,
zero-call benefit routing takes 10.19\,ms versus A-only's 7.78\,ms: fewer
second-model calls do not eliminate selector cost. These sequential laptop
measurements are not confidence intervals for small timing differences.

\subsection{Frozen Final Accuracy}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} shows substantial transfer failure. Holdout
CosPlace R@1 is 14.15\%, EigenPlaces 10.00\%, and DINOv2 3.38\%; the
specialist that was stronger in development is weaker on this block.
EigenPlaces offers only 17 rescue versus 44 harm opportunities, with
541 queries missed by both encoders. Its top-one oracle is 16.77\%.
Externally, CosPlace/EigenPlaces reach 34.50\%/36.00\%, with 31 rescue
and 28 harm opportunities; the oracle is 50.00\%. Oracle gaps measure
available top-one complementarity, not learnable or deployable performance.
All strategies and seeds are retained in the accompanying artifacts.

\subsection{Budget Drift and Target Choice}
With frozen EigenPlaces policies, the nominal 25\% benefit setting calls
B on 78.92\% of holdout queries. Eleven rescues and 36 harms reduce
R@1 to 10.31\%, below A-only and marginally below error prediction's
10.46\%. The corresponding error and gap invocation fractions are
82.00\% and 65.08\%, not equal test costs. We therefore compare the
observed accuracy--cost points rather than asserting a matched test budget.
The nominal 10\% and 50\% benefit settings transfer to 40.15\% and
82.46\% invocation (Table~\ref{tab:final}).

In Gardens Point, the nominal 25\% benefit setting instead calls 15.50\%
of queries. Six rescues and six harms leave R@1 at 34.50\%; error
prediction calls 11.00\% and reaches 34.00\%. Benefit at nominal 50\%
reaches 36.50\% with 29.50\% invocation, only one additional correct
query over B-only. Removing standard deviation also reaches 36.50\%
at 23.00\% invocation, whereas rescue-only at 25\% reaches 34.50\%.
The feature and target ablations do not establish a uniform advantage.
Five-seed nominal-random R@1 is $13.32\pm0.37$\% on the holdout and
$35.20\pm1.60$\% externally; these are population SDs across seeds,
not confidence intervals. Validation-matched random and nominal-random
use the same EigenPlaces validation fraction, but neither enforces the
learned gate's shifted test fraction.

\subsection{Original Pair and Tolerance Sensitivity}
The original DINOv2 pair demonstrates a narrower benefit of the target.
The holdout has eight rescue and 78 harm opportunities. Benefit at nominal
25\% makes two calls, neither changing correctness, and retains A's
14.15\% R@1. Error prediction calls 82.00\%, rescues six and damages
65, reducing R@1 to 5.08\%. Externally, benefit makes 18 calls with five
rescues and three harms, reaching 35.50\%, versus error's 35.00\%.
Avoiding calls to a weak model is useful, but preserving A-only accuracy
does not establish improvement over A or superiority at equal test cost.

Tolerance changes remain separately labeled, using identical frozen
rankings and masks. At Nordland radii one/ten, A-only R@1 is
5.08\%/19.69\%, and EigenPlaces benefit-25 is 4.62\%/13.08\%.
For Gardens Point's custom radius-eight positives, A/EigenPlaces/
benefit-25/error-25 reach 97.00\%/91.00\%/93.00\%/94.00\%.
This differs markedly from exact-index results and is not the tutorial's
soft-ignore evaluation. Fine frame-index performance should not be
equated with geographic localization. Examples are the first query in
manifest order of each correctness class, not the most attractive cases;
their IDs, descriptor samples, scores and rankings are supplied.

\subsection{Real Cost and Replay Checks}
On the EigenPlaces holdout, A-only has the highest observed R@1 and the
lowest measured mean, 7.56\,ms; benefit-25 takes 13.62\,ms and B-only
10.66\,ms. Externally, benefit-25 takes 7.97\,ms versus A-only's
5.84\,ms and B-only's 8.92\,ms, with the accuracy tradeoff above.
Benefit-50 exceeds B-only by one correct query but takes 9.09\,ms,
providing no observed mean-latency saving. Figure~\ref{fig:cost} presents
actual forwards, not cached descriptor timings. Unlocked clocks and
sequential policy execution prevent attributing every timing difference
to the target or claiming stable fine-scale speedups. A reduced number
of second-model calls alone is not an end-to-end cost guarantee.

Independent replay checks all 85000 final offline rows, 107100 real
online rows and 510 strategy--radius evaluations. Descriptors are checked
against model-state and manifest hashes, row IDs, unit norms and array
hashes. Each timed pass verifies the causal decision and complete ranking
against its frozen reference outside the timer. No final fitting or
threshold calibration occurs. Gallery building and warm-file setup costs
are recorded separately; both models remain resident for conditional
execution, so invocation reduction is not a model-memory reduction.

\section{Discussion}
The benefit target separates two risks that A-error prediction conflates,
but the estimate is useful only if those conditional relationships transfer.
EigenPlaces rescue prevalence changes from 51/300 validation queries to
17/650 holdout queries, while harm opportunities remain material.
A-only score distributions also change: the median top1--top2 gap falls
from 0.01477 to 0.00500, and median top-ten standard deviation from
0.02477 to 0.00230. Similar top-one scores can coexist with poor
frame-index correctness. These are descriptive associations, not causal
proof that any single statistic explains the failure.

Repeated and visually similar station frames constrain the information
available to image-only retrieval. Exact Gardens Point frames penalize
nearby references that may depict similar places. We retain all queries
and report the predeclared tolerances rather than widening the primary
criterion after seeing results. Small fixed-route datasets, correlated
frames, low-resolution adaptation, linear gates and unlocked laptop clocks
limit generalization. The missing verified SU implementation limits the
uncertainty comparison. We introduce neither correction/damage labels
nor a universally better algorithm, and make no open-set, metric-pose,
SLAM or robot-deployment claim.

\section{Conclusion}
In this frozen-encoder study, predicting net correction can avoid damaging
calls to a weak second model, but neither reliable accuracy gain nor
validation-budget transfer follows. A development-selected specialist
does not preserve its advantage on a held-out route block. Reporting actual
invocation, signed corrections and real latency exposes these failures
more clearly than invocation budgets or validation Recall alone.
The results motivate testing complementarity and budget transfer before
deploying conditional retrieval; they do not establish universal benefit.
